# Awesome Lucid Dreams [![Awesome](https://awesome.re/badge.svg)](https://awesome.re)

A [lucid dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucid_dream) is a dream in which the dreamer realizes they are dreaming. Lucid dreaming is studied in oneirology, the science of dreams, and is a skill that can be trained.

## Open-Source Projects

- [Dreamento](https://github.com/dreamento/dreamento) - Python toolbox for dream engineering that records, monitors and analyzes EEG in real time.
- [Dream Journal App](https://github.com/gitbrent/dream-journal-app) - A Dream Journal that uses Google Drive.
- [lucidsourcekit](https://github.com/BitFlaker/lucidsourcekit) - An all-you-need toolkit for lucid dreaming including a binaural beats player and much more.
- [Lightened Dream](https://github.com/lucidcode/Lightened-Dream) - A dream journal designed for lucid dreamers.
- [Dormio](https://christinatchen.github.io/dormio/) - Influences the semi-lucid state of mind known as hypnagogia, which occurs just before falling asleep, by playing audio cues.
- [SSILD Trainer](https://github.com/rochismo/ssild-react) - A SSILD (Senses Initiated Lucid Dream) training web app designed to help you practice the SSILD cycles.
- [TLR](https://github.com/rmallela26/TLR) - Open-source iOS/watchOS app for targeted lucidity reactivation, with Apple Watch sleep staging to time cues to REM sleep.
- [YASA](https://github.com/raphaelvallat/yasa) - Python package for sleep analysis, including automatic sleep staging of polysomnography recordings.
- [ezscore](https://github.com/coonwg1/ezscore) - Artifact-aware sleep stage classifiers for forehead EEG headbands such as the ZMax.

## Research Studies

- [Lucid Dreaming Verified by Volitional Communication During REM Sleep (LaBerge, S., et al. 1981)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24171230/) - Provided the first physiological verification of lucid dreaming using pre-agreed eye movement signals during REM sleep.
- [Lucid Dreaming Treatment for Nightmares: A Pilot Study (Spoormaker, V. I., & van den Bout, J. 2006)](https://doi.org/10.1159/000095446) - Randomized 23 nightmare sufferers to individual, group or waiting-list conditions; one 2-hour lucid dreaming treatment session reduced nightmare frequency at 12 weeks, but becoming lucid was not necessary for the improvement.
- [Induction of Lucid Dreams: A Systematic Review of Evidence (Stumbrys, T., et al. 2012)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053810012001614) - Reviewed 35 studies of lucid dream induction techniques and found that none were verified to induce lucid dreams reliably and consistently, although some looked promising.
- [Neural Correlates of Dream Lucidity Obtained From Contrasting Lucid Versus Non-Lucid REM Sleep: A Combined EEG/fMRI Case Study (Dresler, M., et al. 2012)](https://doi.org/10.5665/sleep.1974) - In the one participant (of four) with lucid REM episodes long enough to analyze, lucid dreaming activated the precuneus, parietal lobules, and prefrontal and occipito-temporal cortices compared with non-lucid REM sleep.
- [Measuring Consciousness in Dreams: The Lucidity and Consciousness in Dreams Scale (Voss, U., et al. 2013)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2012.11.001) - Developed the LuCiD scale, with eight factors including insight, control, thought, memory and dissociation, now a standard outcome measure in lucid dreaming studies.
- [Induction of Self Awareness in Dreams Through Frontal Low Current Stimulation of Gamma Activity (Voss, U., et al. 2014)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3719) - Reported that 25 and 40 Hz transcranial alternating current stimulation (tACS) during REM sleep induced self-reflective awareness in dreams, while other frequencies did not.
- [Metacognitive Mechanisms Underlying Lucid Dreaming (Filevich, E., Dresler, M., et al. 2015)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3342-14.2015) - Found greater frontopolar (BA9/10) gray matter volume in people reporting high dream lucidity, and stronger activation of the same region when they monitored their own thoughts while awake.
- [Lucid Dreaming Incidence: A Quality Effects Meta-Analysis of 50 Years of Research (Saunders, D. T., et al. 2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2016.06.002) - Estimated that 55% of people have had at least one lucid dream and 23% have them monthly or more.
- [Effectiveness of Motor Practice in Lucid Dreams: A Comparison With Physical and Mental Practice (Stumbrys, T., Erlacher, D., & Schredl, M. 2016)](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2015.1030342) - In 68 participants, practicing finger tapping in a lucid dream improved next-morning performance about as much as physical or waking mental practice.
- [Reality Testing and the Mnemonic Induction of Lucid Dreams: Findings From the National Australian Lucid Dream Induction Study (Adventure-Heart, D. J., et al. 2017)](https://doi.org/10.1037/drm0000059) - Compared reality testing, WBTB and MILD in 169 participants and found that combining all three induced lucid dreams, especially when participants fell asleep soon after practicing MILD.
- [Lucid Dreaming: Intensity, But Not Frequency, Is Inversely Related to Psychopathology (Aviram, L., & Soffer-Dudek, N. 2018)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00384) - In 187 students, lucid dream frequency was unrelated to psychopathology, while deliberate induction predicted increases in dissociation and schizotypy over two months.
- [Pre-Sleep Treatment With Galantamine Stimulates Lucid Dreaming: A Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Crossover Study (LaBerge, S., LaMarca, K., & Baird, B. 2018)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0201246) - In 121 participants combining WBTB and MILD, 4 mg and 8 mg of galantamine, a prescription drug, raised the share reporting a lucid dream to 27% and 42%, compared with 14% on placebo.
- [Frequent Lucid Dreaming Associated With Increased Functional Connectivity Between Frontopolar Cortex and Temporoparietal Association Areas (Baird, B., et al. 2018)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-36190-w) - Found stronger resting-state connectivity between the anterior prefrontal cortex and temporoparietal regions in frequent lucid dreamers, but no differences in brain structure.
- [The Cognitive Neuroscience of Lucid Dreaming (Baird, B., Mota-Rolim, S. A., & Dresler, M. 2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2019.03.008) - Reviewed EEG, neuroimaging, lesion, drug and brain stimulation studies, finding EEG results mostly underpowered and mixed, prefrontal and parietal regions implicated, and cholinergic stimulation combined with mental set training the most promising induction approach.
- [Findings From the International Lucid Dream Induction Study (Adventure-Heart, D. J. 2020)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01746) - Compared five combinations of reality testing, WBTB, MILD and SSILD in 355 participants and found MILD and SSILD similarly effective, regardless of prior lucid dreaming experience.
- [Wake Up, Work on Dreams, Back to Bed and Lucid Dream: A Sleep Laboratory Study (Erlacher, D., & Stumbrys, T. 2020)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01383) - Waking participants after six hours to practice MILD for 30 to 60 minutes produced lucid dreams in 36 to 54% of unselected participants, compared with 9% after reading.
- [Attempted Induction of Signalled Lucid Dreaming by Transcranial Alternating Current Stimulation (Blanchette-Carrière, C., et al. 2020)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2020.102957) - Repeated tACS lucid dream induction with stricter methods and found signal-verified lucid dreams after both real and sham stimulation.
- [Real-Time Dialogue Between Experimenters and Dreamers During REM Sleep (Konkoly, K. R., et al. 2021)](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822%2821%2900059-2) - Demonstrated that real-time communication with lucid dreamers is possible via eye movements and facial muscle signals.
- [Two-Way Communication in Lucid REM Sleep Dreaming (Baird, B., LaBerge, S., & Tononi, G. 2021)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2021.04.004) - Commentary on Konkoly et al. 2021 that discusses whether longer two-way conversations with lucid dreamers are feasible.
- [Lucid Dreaming Occurs in Activated Rapid Eye Movement Sleep, Not a Mixture of Sleep and Wakefulness (Baird, B., Tononi, G., & LaBerge, S. 2022)](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsab294) - Found that signal-verified lucid dreams occur during REM sleep with higher eye movement density, and that the reported 40 Hz "hybrid state" signal was an eye movement artifact.
- [Benefits and Concerns of Seeking and Experiencing Lucid Dreams: Benefits Are Tied to Successful Induction and Dream Control (Mallett, R., et al. 2022)](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleepadvances/zpac027) - Analyzed posts from a large lucid dreaming forum and found that lucid dreams can end nightmares, while negative outcomes mostly followed failed induction attempts or low dream control.
- [Combining Presleep Cognitive Training and REM-Sleep Stimulation in a Laboratory Morning Nap for Lucid Dream Induction (Carr, M., et al. 2023)](https://doi.org/10.1037/cns0000227) - Introduced targeted lucidity reactivation (TLR): participants learned to associate sound and light cues with a lucid mindset before a morning nap, and replaying the cues in REM sleep produced signal-verified lucid dreams in 50% of participants, compared with 17% without cueing.
- [A Systematic Review of New Empirical Data on Lucid Dream Induction Techniques (Tan, S., & Fan, J. 2023)](https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.13786) - Updated Stumbrys et al. 2012 with 19 newer studies covering 14 techniques and found MILD the most effective, with SSILD and galantamine promising but needing replication.
- [The Effectiveness of Lucid Dreaming Therapy in Patients With Nightmares: A Systematic Review (Ouchene, R., et al. 2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.encep.2023.01.008) - Found 4 randomized controlled trials and several case studies; most reported fewer nightmares in adults, but internal validity was limited.
- [Targeted Lucidity Reactivation Implemented in an Open-Source watchOS App (Mallela, R., & Mallett, R. 2024)](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/article/view/100797) - Developed an open-source iOS/watchOS application that runs targeted lucidity reactivation at home, using Apple Watch motion and heart rate data to time cues to REM sleep.
- [Provoking Lucid Dreams at Home With Sensory Cues Paired With Pre-Sleep Cognitive Training (Konkoly, K. R., et al. 2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2024.103759) - Moved targeted lucidity reactivation (TLR) to a smartphone without polysomnography. Lucid dreaming increased compared with the prior week and with blinded control nights, but only when the sleep cues matched the pre-sleep training sounds.
- [The Clinical Neuroscience of Lucid Dreaming (Tzioridou, S., et al. 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106011) - Reviewed links between lucid dreaming and nightmare disorder, depression, anxiety, psychosis and dissociation, and the potential drawbacks of lucid dreaming interventions.
- [Electrophysiological Correlates of Lucid Dreaming: Sensor and Source Level Signatures (Demirel, Ç., et al. 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2237-24.2025) - Pooled EEG across labs and found minimal sensor-level differences between lucid and non-lucid REM sleep, but reduced right parietal beta power and more alpha and gamma connectivity during lucid dreaming.
- [Inducing Lucid Dreaming With Multisensory Stimulation: A Preregistered Multi-Center Study (Picard-Deland, C., Jafarzadeh Esfahani, M., et al. 2026)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.21.600133v2) - Preprint. Combined pre-sleep SSILD training with light, sound and vibration cues during REM sleep in 60 participants across three labs. Cueing did not make lucid dreams more frequent than sham, but roughly doubled the duration of signal-verified lucid dreams.

## Reality Checks

Reality checks are tests that are performed to check if you are dreaming.

- Finger Through Hand - Push your index finger through the palm of the other hand.
- How Did I Get Here - Recall the steps that got you to your current location.
- Nose Pinch - Squeeze your nose closed with your index finger and thumb and try to take a breath.
- Finger Counting - Look closely at your hands for any unusual patterns and count your fingers.
- Levitation - Jump or try to float; in a dream you will often hover or rise.
